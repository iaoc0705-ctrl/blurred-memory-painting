Primary request: A quiet painting with no people at all. One plain wooden chair has been left alone on a stone quay in sea fog, turned to face the water. It must read as a chair at a glance: four straight legs, a flat seat, a low upright back, seen from the side, in clear silhouette against the pale fog. The chair is small, about one twelfth of the image height, and slightly off-centre. No other object of any kind is present.
Style/medium: Original oil on canvas, matte and chalky, dry-brush and scumbled strokes. The chair's outline is open - its legs and back dissolve into the fog, so it reads as a soft shape rather than a cut-out. Broad dragged paint, visible canvas weave, uneven finish - some passages wiped down and left raw. A painted memory, not a photograph.
Composition/framing: Portrait 4:5. Water and fog occupy more than four fifths of the image. The quay is a single simple dark horizontal band with a straight edge across the lower third - flat and quiet, not a pile of rubble or rocks.
Lighting/mood: White sea fog with almost no contrast and a pale diffuse light band lying low over the water. Quiet, distant, unresolved - the feeling of a place remembered years later.
Color: chalk white, bone, grey-blue and a faint olive, all muted and greyed; exactly one small warm accent in the whole image - the faded rust-ochre of the chair, no larger than a brush stroke.
Constraints: no people and no animals anywhere; no readable text, sign, boat name, logo or signature; completely original subject and composition.
